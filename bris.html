What Is a Bris?

A plain-language guide to Brit Milah: its meaning, timing, the process itself, medical benefits, how to prepare, and what healing looks like afterward.

The meaning

An eternal covenant, not just a procedure.

ברית — יותר מנוהל רפואי

A bris, formally called a Brit Milah (Hebrew for "covenant of circumcision"), is the ritual circumcision performed on a Jewish baby boy on the eighth day of his life. Its origin is Genesis 17:10–11, where G-d instructs Abraham that circumcision will be "the sign of a covenant" between G-d and Abraham's descendants — a covenant that has been kept, unbroken, for thousands of years.

That distinction matters. A bris is not simply a medical circumcision that happens to be performed by a Jewish practitioner. It is a religious milestone: the moment a baby boy formally enters the covenant of Abraham, marked with blessings, family, and community — not just a clinical appointment.

"He shall be circumcised... an everlasting covenant between Me and you and your offspring after you."

Genesis 17:11–13

Rabbi Yossi treats every bris this way — as a sacred family occasion he is honored to be part of, guided with the same warmth whether it's a family's first bris or their fifth.

Timing and process

What actually happens, and when.

מהלך הברית ומועדה
I.

The eighth day, without exception

Jewish law fixes the bris on the baby's eighth day of life, counting his birth day as day one. The bris happens on the eighth day even if it falls on Shabbat or a Jewish holiday. The only reason to delay is a medical one — jaundice, prematurity, or anything else a pediatrician flags — in which case the bris waits until the doctor confirms the baby is ready.

II.

Who's in the room

A bris is a family and community event, typically held in the morning at home or at a synagogue. Traditionally a Sandek holds the baby during the bris itself (often a grandparent), and a Kvatter and Kvatterin carry the baby into the room. Rabbi Yossi walks first-time parents through each role beforehand so nothing feels unfamiliar on the day.

III.

The procedure itself

Rabbi Yossi performs the circumcision freehand, without clamping — widely considered the most natural method. The procedure itself takes only moments, with blessings recited immediately before and after. Most babies settle within minutes.

IV.

Right after

A festive meal (סעודת מצווה) traditionally follows, giving family and friends a chance to celebrate together. Rabbi Yossi checks on the baby before leaving and remains reachable for any question that comes up that day.

Why a Mohel

Specialized skill, and the training to do it right.

מדוע לבחור במוהל מוסמך

A Mohel is trained specifically in the physiology and technique of circumcision, performing it far more often than a general practitioner, alongside the religious training to conduct the bris as the ritual it is. The combination is the point: technical precision from repetition, plus the halachic knowledge to do it correctly and meaningfully.

Rabbi Yossi's approach is freehand, without clamping — a quick, natural technique associated with minimal pain and fast healing. Families consistently describe the experience as calmer and more personal than a hospital circumcision, with direct access to Rabbi Yossi before and after, not a rotating hospital staff.

Medical benefits

The health case for circumcision.

היתרונות הרפואיים

Beyond its religious significance, circumcision carries documented medical benefits that lead many pediatricians to recommend it independent of religious practice:

01Reduced risk of urinary tract infections, especially in the first year of life.
02Lower transmission rates of certain sexually transmitted infections later in life.
03Near elimination of the already-rare risk of penile cancer.
04Reduced risk of balanitis and balanoposthitis (inflammation of the glans and foreskin).
05Simpler long-term hygiene.

Source: American Academy of Pediatrics, Circumcision Policy Statement.

Preparing

What to do before the day arrives.

הכנות לקראת הברית

Most of the preparation is simple, and Rabbi Yossi handles the logistics with you:

I.

Call as soon as the baby is born

Reach out right away so the eighth-day date is locked in and Rabbi Yossi can plan around it, including travel if you're outside Las Vegas or Henderson.

II.

Pediatrician clearance if needed

If the baby was premature, jaundiced, or had a complicated delivery, Rabbi Yossi will coordinate directly with your pediatrician on timing.

III.

Choose a space

Home or synagogue both work well — what matters is good lighting and a flat, stable surface. Rabbi Yossi can advise on setup when he visits beforehand.

IV.

Decide on roles

Choose a Sandek and, if you'd like, a Kvatter and Kvatterin. Rabbi Yossi explains what each role involves so honored family members know exactly what to expect.

Aftercare

Healing is quick, and you're not on your own.

טיפול לאחר הברית

Most babies heal quickly and without complication. Before he leaves, Rabbi Yossi gives clear, specific instructions on diapering, cleaning, and what's normal versus what to call about — then follows up personally in the days after to check on the baby directly, not just by form letter.

That follow-up is a deliberate part of how Rabbi Yossi practices: parents consistently mention it as one of the things that put them most at ease, especially with a first child. If anything looks off during healing, he's a call or WhatsApp message away.
